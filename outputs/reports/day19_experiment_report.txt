1. OBJECTIVE
------------------------------------------------------------
The objective of this experiment was to identify potential
MITRE ATT&CK techniques associated with correlated security
alert pairs using Python-based mapping logic.

The experiment also aimed to distinguish potential
technique mappings from cases where available evidence
was insufficient.

7. Validated technique IDs and output completeness.

9. LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
- The experiment uses only four synthetic security events.
- Technique mappings are based on event descriptions.
- PowerShell process creation does not prove malicious activity.
- Individual authentication failures do not prove brute force.
- Correlated alerts do not automatically represent one attack.
- Further validation using command-line details, process
  telemetry and authentication history is required.

10. CONCLUSION
------------------------------------------------------------
The Day 19 experiment successfully implemented a
Python-based MITRE ATT&CK technique mapping process.

The framework identified two potential event-level
T1059.001 mappings and correctly avoided assigning
T1110 without sufficient evidence.

The experiment demonstrates how explainable mapping
logic can support SOC alert investigation while
reducing unsupported technique classifications.
